The interview block began at **6:00 AM**. The candidate had **around 5–6 interviews** and was told that a smaller number does not mean rejection: GS is trying to find the department that fits you.

## Logistics

- Interviews start on time. Be punctual and sleep properly. The candidate had another company's test until 2 AM and woke at 5:35 AM; the panel was understanding, but it is not something to plan for.
- Panels did not let the candidate leave for other companies in between. The candidate reads that as a good sign: if you are called back, you are still in. If you are not, do not wait around, since GS will not tell you to leave.
- If you want an interview, **get in touch with the organiser on the company's side**.

## Interview by interview

### Interview 1: convex hull, then a game

- **Convex hull**: connect points so the closed figure has the least area and encloses all the points. The candidate could not answer at 6 AM, and only realised later it was a standard problem.
- The interviewer then moved to a simpler one: the **number game to 20** (pick 1–3 each turn, first to reach 20 wins). The strategy is worked in [Questions asked](../questions-asked).

### Interview 2: statistics and the internship

- Calculating **variance**, the **Gaussian distribution**, and questions about the internship.

### Interview 3: matrices and a puzzle

- A **matrix problem** where each element was the sum of the row number squared and e raised to the column number. It led to a proof about the equality of sums and then a generalisation to a function of row and column numbers. The candidate does not remember the exact statement.
- A **two-carts puzzle** with a sensor under a track: how to make the carts collide when you only control them as they pass the sensor.
- The panel was helpful, and the candidate's advice: **talk to them throughout, they help you out a lot**.
- They were interested in the **low CPI**.

### Interview 4: senior panel, resume deep-dive

- General questions about internships and interests, with particular interest in the candidate's stated interest in **finance and global markets** (advice: don't fake it; build the habit and it pays off in every interview).
- The **Yelp dataset challenge** and **NLP** from an AI project.
- Basic questions on **scikit-learn** libraries.
- The **flash crash** in the stock market, tied to an internship project.

### Interview 5: a senior from the candidate's own department, now at GS

- Asked whether the candidate still had interest in their own department (Electrical), which played no role in the process.
- A **data-structures question**, then the **row-sorted, column-sorted matrix search**. The candidate started from the highest-complexity approach, worked down, and needed a hint for the best one. The approaches are in [Questions asked](../questions-asked).

## How it ended

The interviews ran **until about 11:30 AM**. The candidate was then called in and told GS was "interested" and would like to "meet" soon. They were asked whether they would be appearing for other companies, and gave an honest answer.

## Candidate's takeaways

- Think aloud and ask for hints when stuck. Interviewers helped when the candidate could not articulate a problem.
- Start from a simple approach and improve it step by step. Walking through increasing efficiency was received well.
- Be honest about other processes you are in.
