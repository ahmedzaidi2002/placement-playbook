### 1 · Q31-34, statement and assumptions

*Asked by: Accenture (Previous year)*

Answer A if only I is implicit, B if only II, C if either, D if neither, E if both.
(31) "It is desirable to put a child in school at about 5." I: at that age the child is ready
to learn. II: schools do not admit children after six. (32) "The government has decided to
reduce customs duty on computer peripherals." I: domestic prices may go up. II: domestic
manufacturers may oppose it. (33) "As demand is great, every person will be given only five
tickets." I: the organisers are not keen to sell. II: no one wants more than five. (34) "The
railways will raise freight charges by 10% to avoid a loss this year." I: freight volume may
stay the same. II: the extra revenue may offset part or all of the deficit.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answers: (31) A (32) D (33) D (34) B**

**Why.**
- (31) Recommending school at about 5 assumes the child is ready to learn then (I). It does not assume schools refuse children over six (II).
- (32) A duty cut lowers import prices; neither "domestic prices may rise" nor "domestic makers may oppose" is something the decision must assume.
- (33) Limiting tickets because demand is high does not assume reluctance to sell or that nobody wants more than five.
- (34) Raising freight to avoid a loss assumes the extra revenue will help cover the deficit (II). (I is arguably implicit too, but the key takes B.)

**Concept: implicit assumption.** An assumption is something the speaker must take for granted for the statement to make sense. Test: if the assumption were false, would the statement become pointless? If yes, it is implicit. A possible consequence of the statement is not an assumption.

### 2 · completing school by 18

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Statement: It is better for a kid to complete schooling by the age of 18. Which
assumption is implicit? I. Colleges do not consider candidates after 18 years of age.
II. The kid must be admitted to college at the age of 18.

- **(a)** Only (I)
- **(b)** Only (II)
- **(c)** Neither (I) nor (II)
- **(d)** Both (I) and (II)

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) neither**

**Why.** "It is better to finish school by 18" is a general recommendation. It does not require that colleges reject over-18s (I), nor that the child must join college at exactly 18 (II). Both are extreme readings.

**Concept: watch for absolute words.** Assumptions with "must", "only", "do not consider" are usually too strong for a mild statement like "it is better to".

### 3 · inference, the foreign tourist

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Statement: A foreign tourist was bound and beaten by a group of villagers after the
villagers suspected him of misbehaving with local women. The district administration has
immediately sent a team to the spot to take strict action against the culprits. Which
inference can be drawn?

- **(a)** A foreign tourist was drunk.
- **(b)** The root cause of such incidents is language barrier.
- **(c)** The villagers are irked by the presence of foreigners in their areas.
- **(d)** The government generally protects foreign tourists.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) the government generally protects foreign tourists**

**Why.** The administration acted immediately against the attackers, which suggests protection of tourists. Nothing says he was drunk (a), that language caused it (b), or that villagers dislike foreigners in general (c).

**Concept: an inference must follow from the text.** Pick the option that the facts support most directly; reject anything that adds new facts or generalises from one incident.

### 4 · mother scolds, hide or complain

*Asked by: Protium Finance (OA 2025)*

Read the scenario and determine the logical link between the conditions and the options.
"Whenever my mother scolds me, I either hide behind my father or complain to my grandma."

- **(a)** If I complain to my grandma or I hide behind my father, then my mother must have scolded me.
- **(b)** If I did not complain to my grandma and I did not hide behind my father, then my mother must not have scolded me.
- **(c)** If my mother does not scold me, I will neither hide behind my father nor complain to my grandma.
- **(d)** None of the mentioned options are correct.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b)**

**Why.** The statement is "scolds → (hide or complain)". Its contrapositive, "not hide and not complain → not scolded", is logically equivalent, and that is (b). (a) is the converse and (c) the inverse; neither follows.

**Concept: contrapositive.** For "if P then Q":
- Contrapositive "if not Q then not P" is always equivalent.
- Converse "if Q then P" and inverse "if not P then not Q" are not.
When Q is "A or B", "not Q" is "not A and not B" (De Morgan's law).

### 5 · course of action for low marks

*Asked by: Sigmoid (Campus hiring 2026)*

Situation: a student is consistently getting low marks in mathematics. Courses of action:
(1) The student should be provided with extra tutoring or special classes. (2) The student
should be advised to stop studying mathematics altogether.

- **(a)** if only 1 follows
- **(b)** if only 2 follows
- **(c)** if either 1 or 2 follows
- **(d)** if neither 1 nor 2 follows
- **(e)** if both 1 and 2 follow

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) only 1 follows**

**Why.** Extra tutoring addresses the cause (weak understanding). Stopping maths altogether gives up instead of fixing anything, and is impractical.

**Concept: course of action.** A valid action should solve or reduce the problem and be practical. Reject extreme, unrelated or defeatist actions.

### 6 · drip irrigation

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IISc, second set)*

Gardener: install drip irrigation besides the sprinklers, so the lawns do not dry out if
someone forgets to water them; this costs less than redoing damaged lawns. Owner: it is
expensive and unnecessary. Which choice best supports the gardener?

- **(a)** The use of sprinklers can still reduce the longevity of the lawns due to underwatering
- **(b)** Severe rains can rot the lawns
- **(c)** Snow storms can damage the lawns
- **(d)** Drip irrigation can save the lawns when nobody is there to water them

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a)** (the best-supported reading)

**Why.** The owner says drip irrigation is unnecessary because sprinklers exist. (a) answers exactly that: even with sprinklers, underwatering can still shorten the lawns' life, so the backup has value. (d) just repeats the gardener's own claim; (b) and (c) are damage drip irrigation cannot prevent.

**Concept: strengthen an argument by answering the objection.** The best support closes the gap the other side points to. Restating the conclusion adds nothing.

### 7 · mall elevators

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IIT BHU 2023)*

Elevators E1, E3, E5 stop at even floors and E2, E4 at odd floors; all stop at the first and
seventh floors. Which must be true? (1) From the ground floor, E1 cannot take you to the fifth
floor. (2) From the sixth floor, E3 cannot take you to the ninth. (3) Going from an even floor
to an odd floor needs a change of elevator. (4) None of these.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (4) none of these**

**Why.** All elevators stop at floors 1 and 7, which are odd. So E1 (an "even" elevator) can still go from the ground floor up to 7 and you can reach odd floors without a change: (3) is not a must. (1) and (2) are only true for a direct ride and are not forced either.

**Concept: "must be true".** A statement must be true only if no arrangement consistent with the facts breaks it. One counterexample (E1 stopping at 7) is enough to reject it.

### 8 · an energy-drink argument

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (NIT Calicut 2023)*

50 consumers were given a brand's herbal energy drink in time-bound situations and 80% said
it helped their productivity; "hence it is the best energy drink in the market". The
argument is most vulnerable because it fails to consider:

- **(a)** that some participants were allergic to herbs
- **(b)** that the herbs are not harmful
- **(c)** that the price of the drink is high
- **(d)** whether the consumers had tasted other brands

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) whether the consumers had tasted other brands**

**Why.** "Best in the market" is a comparison with other drinks, but the study only tested this one brand. Without comparing, the conclusion does not follow.

**Concept: find the gap between evidence and conclusion.** The evidence (80% felt it helped) supports "it helps", not "it is the best". A flaw question asks what the argument ignores; here, a comparison group.

### 9 · mud on the sleeves

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IIT ISM 2024)*

All who bike from A to B get mud on their sleeves. What is needed to conclude that Keith
travelled from A to B?

- **(a)** Keith has mud on his sleeves
- **(b)** Keith travelled from A
- **(c)** Keith travelled to B
- **(d)** None

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) none**

**Why.** "Biking from A to B → mud on sleeves." Mud on Keith's sleeves (a) does not prove he made that trip: mud could come from anywhere. Knowing he travelled from A (b) or to B (c) alone does not complete the trip either.

**Concept: affirming the consequent is a fallacy.** From "if P then Q" and "Q", you cannot conclude P. Only "P" gives "Q" (modus ponens), and "not Q" gives "not P".

### 10 · an online learning platform

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (BITS Pilani)*

Monthly members get fewer amenities; users would pay more for better ones, covering the cost,
yet the platform wants profit and has not improved them. Which explains the decision?

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) very few users leave because of the missing amenities**

**Why.** If hardly anyone leaves over the amenities, improving them gains the platform almost nothing, even if users would pay a bit more. That explains why a profit-seeking platform does nothing.

**Concept: resolve the paradox.** The best explanation makes both facts true at once (users would pay more, yet the company does not act). Look for the option that removes the company's incentive.

### 11 · university applications (1)

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

The graph shows the undergraduate applicants to Ralph University (thousands) and the
amount spent processing them (thousand \$):
|  | 2009 | 2010 | 2011 | 2012 | 2013 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Applicants (000s) | 35 | 15 | 20 | 40 | 35 |
| Processing expenses (000s \$) | 4 | 8 | 5 | 3 | 9 |
Over the five years, on average, paper costs were 30% of the processing outlay, staff
salaries 45% and marketing 25%. Assume the revenue per applicant has not changed.

Select Yes if the statement is definitely true, otherwise No. *Statement:* In
2014, if the total number of applications is limited to 30{,}000 and processing costs
are held to \$5{,}000, the university will achieve a higher revenue total than in any of
the previous five years.

- **(a)** Yes
- **(b)** No

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) No**

**Why.** Revenue per applicant is constant, so revenue is proportional to applicants. 30,000 applicants is fewer than 2009 (35k), 2012 (40k) and 2013 (35k), so revenue would be lower than those years. Holding costs to \$5000 changes profit, not revenue.

**Concept: revenue vs profit.** Revenue is money coming in; profit is revenue minus costs. A statement about revenue cannot be helped by cutting costs.

### 12 · university applications (2)

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Same data as the previous question. Select Yes if the statement is definitely true,
otherwise No. *Statement:* Given the trend over the past five years, when the total
number of applicants increases, the cost of processing the applications decreases, and
vice versa.

- **(a)** Yes
- **(b)** No

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) Yes** (as the test intends)

**Why.** Every year-on-year change moves the two series in opposite directions: 2009→10 applicants down, cost up; 2010→11 up, down; 2011→12 up, down; 2012→13 down, up.

**Concept: negative correlation in a trend.** When one series rises each time the other falls, they move inversely. (With only five points this is a trend, not a law, but the data never contradicts it.)

### 13 · Ethan's trips

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Whenever Ethan drives a truck from Chicago to Michigan City, he also visits Old
St. Patrick's Church and Promontory Point Park. When he visits the church, he eats at the
bakery close to it; the bakery is famous in Michigan City for its blueberry cheesecake.
Ethan sometimes donates money to the church-run school. We also know that Ethan ate at the
bakery close to the church in June.

*Statement:* Ethan visited Old St. Patrick's Church in June. Is it definitely true,
could it be true, or can it not be true?

- **(a)** Is definitely true
- **(b)** Could be true
- **(c)** Cannot be true

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) could be true**

**Why.** We know "church visit → eats at the bakery", and that he ate at the bakery in June. That does not prove he visited the church: he might have gone to the bakery alone. Nothing rules it out either.

**Concept: one-way implications.** "If P then Q" plus "Q" leaves P possible but not certain. Classify as "definitely true" only if it follows, "cannot be true" only if it contradicts the facts.

### 14 · actors' obligations

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Movie actors are bound by legal, ethical and contractual obligations to the movie's
investors: to be professional, finish the shoot on time, and not reveal details of the
movie without the director's consent. Any breach can lead to the actor being sued by the
production house, dismissed from the film, or having a long-term contract with the
production company rescinded. What inference can be drawn?

- **(a)** Both the actors' finances and their reputation will suffer if they breach their obligations.
- **(b)** Contractual penalties ensure that a production house does not have to enforce an actor's professional duties.
- **(c)** The severe penalties for breach of contract serve to deter actors from delaying the movie.
- **(d)** Actors are legally and contractually obligated to the movie's investors but not to the director or their fellow actors.
- **(e)** Lawsuits in the movie industry are mostly between production houses and the actors.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) the severe penalties serve to deter actors from delaying the movie**

**Why.** The passage lists finishing on time as an obligation and lists heavy penalties for breaches; deterrence is the inference closest to the text. (a) adds "reputation" (not mentioned). (b) and (e) go beyond the text. (d) is contradicted: the director's consent is part of the obligations.

**Concept: inference stays inside the text.** Choose the option that needs the fewest outside assumptions. Words not in the passage ("reputation", "mostly") are red flags.

### 15 · strengthen the argument

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

"Five years ago I got a perfect score on the LSAT. Therefore I expect a perfect score
this time as well." Which statement, if true, most strengthens this claim?

- **(a)** Individual LSAT scores have been fairly consistent across multiple evaluations.
- **(b)** No new types of LSAT questions have been introduced in the past five years.
- **(c)** Five years ago, the LSAT was a paper-and-pencil test, and now it is an online test.
- **(d)** When I retook the GRE, my score was exactly the same as in my first attempt.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) individual LSAT scores have been fairly consistent across evaluations**

**Why.** The argument assumes a person's score stays the same over time. (a) supports exactly that. (b) helps a little; (c) weakens it (format changed); (d) is about a different test.

**Concept: strengthen by supporting the hidden assumption.** Find the unstated link between evidence and conclusion (past score predicts future score) and pick the option that makes that link more believable.

### 16 · Sara's typing

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Sara must type an email to a customer and some mathematical equations for a technical
manual. On average she types 35 words per minute and 2 equations per minute. The email
has at most 525 words (emails average 140 words). The manual has 5 sections, each
explaining at most 4 equations; the last manual had 3 equations per section.

Which statement has to be *definitely true* for Sara to commit to delivering the
typed email and equations within 21 minutes?

- **(a)** Introduction text of 150 words has to be copied and pasted from an existing email.
- **(b)** The email is written in points.
- **(c)** The length of the email is restricted to 450 words.
- **(d)** The technical manual has to be released with a lesser number of equations this year.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 150 words of introduction are pasted from an existing email**

**Why.** Worst case today: email 525 words at 35 wpm = 15 min; equations 5 × 4 = 20 at 2 per min = 10 min; total 25 > 21. With 150 words pasted, only 375 are typed: 10.7 + 10 = 20.7 ≤ 21. A 450-word limit (c) still gives 12.9 + 10 = 22.9. (b) and (d) give no guaranteed bound.

**Concept: guarantee means plan for the worst case.** To commit to a deadline you must bound the maximum time, using the upper limits (525 words, 4 equations per section), not the averages.

### 17 · statement and assumption

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

*Statement:* "One of the main purposes of having a democratic society, run by
elected representatives, is to enable individuals to exercise freedom of expression."
*Assumption:* Freedom of expression is a benefit that the author of this statement
highly regards. Is the assumption implicit (Yes/No)?

- **(a)** Yes
- **(b)** No

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) Yes**

**Why.** Calling freedom of expression "one of the main purposes" of democracy only makes sense if the author values it highly.

**Concept: value assumptions.** When someone names something as a key goal, they implicitly regard it as worth having.
