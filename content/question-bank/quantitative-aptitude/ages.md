### 1 · Q18, ages in a family of four

*Asked by: Axxela (NIT Bhopal 2024)*

A family of four: father, mother, son and daughter, in decreasing order of age. The father
is 5 years older than the mother. The total age of the male members exceeds that of the
female members by 15 years, and the children's ages total 20 years. What is the son's age?

- **(a)** 10
- **(b)** 15
- **(c)** 20
- **(d)** 25

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) 15**

**Why.**
1. Males are father F and son S; females are mother M and daughter D. "Males exceed females by 15": (F + S) − (M + D) = 15.
2. Regroup it: (F − M) + (S − D) = 15. We know F − M = 5, so S − D = 10.
3. The children total 20: S + D = 20.
4. Add the last two equations: 2S = 30, so S = 15 (and D = 5).

**Concept: regrouping to cancel unknowns.** Four unknowns look scary, but you never need all four. Group the terms so the facts you were given (F − M = 5) drop straight in. A sum equation plus a difference equation of the same two unknowns always solves in one step: add them to get twice the bigger one.

### 2 · Problem Solving

*Asked by: Qualcomm (60-question online assessment)*

Aman is 9 years older than his brother Rishi. Six years ago, the sum of their ages was 38. What is Aman's current age?

- **(a)** 32.5 years
- **(b)** 34.5 years
- **(c)** 22.5 years
- **(d)** 29.5 years

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) 29.5 years**

**Why.**
1. Six years ago the two ages added to 38. Each person has aged 6 years since, so today's total is 38 + 6 + 6 = 50.
2. Aman = Rishi + 9, so Rishi + 9 + Rishi = 50, giving Rishi = 20.5 and Aman = 29.5.

**Concept: a sum of ages grows by (number of people) × (years passed).** Every person gets one year older each year, so a total of two people's ages rises by 2 per year. Shift the total to "today" first, then use the age gap. The age **difference** never changes over time, which is why "9 years older" holds then and now.

### 3 · father and son

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IIT BHU 2023)*

Two years ago X was six times his son's age; in two years twice X's age will be seven times
the son's. Ratio of present ages?

- **(a)** 13:3
- **(b)** 12:3
- **(c)** 11:4
- **(d)** 3:4

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 13 : 3**

**Why.** Let the son be S and X be X today.
1. Two years ago: X − 2 = 6(S − 2), so X = 6S − 10.
2. In two years: 2(X + 2) = 7(S + 2), so 2X + 4 = 7S + 14.
3. Substitute X: 12S − 20 + 4 = 7S + 14, so 5S = 30, S = 6 and X = 26.
4. Ratio 26 : 6 = 13 : 3.

**Concept: translate each time point into one equation.** Write every age relative to *today*: "n years ago" means subtract n from each person, "in n years" means add n. Two time statements give two equations in two unknowns, which you solve by substitution. Always cancel the ratio at the end.

### 4 · Jane's age

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IISc 2023)*

Five years ago Jane was ten times her niece's age; in five years three times Jane's age will
be 12 times the niece's. Ratio now?

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: 11 : 2** (Jane 55, niece 10)

**Why.** Let Jane be J and the niece N today.
1. Five years ago: J − 5 = 10(N − 5), so J = 10N − 45.
2. In five years: 3(J + 5) = 12(N + 5). Divide by 3: J + 5 = 4(N + 5), so J = 4N + 15.
3. Equate: 10N − 45 = 4N + 15, so N = 10 and J = 55. Ratio 55 : 10 = 11 : 2.

**Concept: simplify each equation before combining.** "Three times Jane's age equals 12 times the niece's" is just "Jane = 4 × niece" in disguise; dividing out the common factor first keeps the numbers small. Then both equations give J in terms of N, and setting them equal finishes it.
