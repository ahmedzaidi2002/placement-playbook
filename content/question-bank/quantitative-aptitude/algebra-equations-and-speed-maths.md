### 1 · speed mathematics, two blocks of six

*Asked by: Axxela (NIT Bhopal 2024)*

Fill in the blanks.
**Block A.** (1) $85 \times 75$; (2) $19+27+24+23+22+28+17$; (3) $x + y = 84$,
$2x - y = 60$, find $xy/144$; (4) $(12000/600)^3$; (5) $4^4 - 4^3 - 4^2 - 4^1 - 4^0$;
(6) $8^3 - 4^4 + 2^4$.
**Block B.** (1) $1728, 1331, 1000, \_\_, 512$; (2) $a^2 + b^2 = 265$, $ab = 132$,
find $(a+b)^2$; (3) $13^2 + 7^3$; (4) $\_\_/9 = 140$; (5) $(72 \times 7 \times 3) -
(50 \times 1.8)$; (6) $x + y + z = 87$, $x + y - z = 33$, find $z(x+y)$.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answers.**
- Block A: 6375; 160; 12; 8000; 171; 272.
- Block B: 729; 529; 512; 1260; 1422; 1620.

**Why (the fast route for each).**
- A1: 85 and 75 sit 5 either side of 80, so 85 × 75 = (80 + 5)(80 − 5) = 80² − 5² = 6400 − 25 = 6375.
- A2: pair into round numbers: 27 + 23 = 50, 28 + 22 = 50, 19 + 24 + 17 = 60, total 160.
- A3: add the equations: 3x = 144, x = 48, y = 36; xy/144 = 1728/144 = 12.
- A4: 12000/600 = 20 and 20³ = 8000.
- A5: 256 − 64 − 16 − 4 − 1 = 171.
- A6: 512 − 256 + 16 = 272.
- B1: 1728, 1331, 1000, __, 512 are 12³, 11³, 10³, ?, 8³, so the gap is 9³ = 729.
- B2: (a + b)² = a² + b² + 2ab = 265 + 264 = 529.
- B3: 169 + 343 = 512. B4: 140 × 9 = 1260. B5: 1512 − 90 = 1422.
- B6: add the equations: 2(x + y) = 120, so x + y = 60 and z = 27; z(x + y) = 1620.

**Concept: speed-maths identities.** Three ideas carry most of these.
- (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²: when two numbers sit equally far from a round number, multiply via squares.
- (a + b)² = a² + b² + 2ab: lets you get a sum's square from the sum of squares and the product.
- Adding or subtracting equations to kill a variable (x + y + z and x + y − z differ only in z).
Spotting a pattern (here, falling cubes) is quicker than any arithmetic.

### 3 · Q13, a square root of a ratio

*Asked by: Axxela (NIT Bhopal 2024)*

$\sqrt{\dfrac{(0.03)^2 + (0.21)^2 + (0.065)^2}{(0.003)^2 + (0.021)^2 + (0.0065)^2}} = {?}$

- **(a)** 1/10
- **(b)** 100
- **(c)** 10
- **(d)** 1/100

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) 10**

**Why.** Compare term by term: 0.03 = 10 × 0.003, 0.21 = 10 × 0.021 and 0.065 = 10 × 0.0065. Squaring, every numerator term is 100 times its denominator partner, so the whole fraction is 100 and √100 = 10.

**Concept: factor out a common scale.** If every term of the top is k times the matching term of the bottom, then (k²a² + k²b² + k²c²)/(a² + b² + c²) = k². You never need to compute the squares. Shifting a decimal point one place is multiplying by 10, and squaring turns that into 100.

### 4 · Q3, cost of running a train

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

The rate of coal consumption of a locomotive varies as the square of its speed and is
1000 kg per hour at 60 km/h. If coal costs Rs 15 per 100 kg and the other expenses
of the train are Rs 12 per hour, find a formula for the cost in paise per kilometre
when the speed is $X$ km/h.

- **(a)** $1200/X + 75X/18$
- **(b)** $1200 + 75X^2/18$
- **(c)** $1200 + 5X^2/18$
- **(d)** None of the above

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 1200/X + 75X/18** paise per km

**Why.**
1. Coal use varies as speed²: rate = kX². At X = 60 it is 1000 kg/h, so k = 1000/3600 and rate = 1000X²/3600 kg per hour.
2. Coal costs Rs 15 per 100 kg = 15 paise per kg, so coal costs 15 × 1000X²/3600 = 25X²/6 paise per hour.
3. Other costs: Rs 12 per hour = 1200 paise per hour.
4. In one hour the train covers X km, so cost per km = (1200 + 25X²/6)/X = 1200/X + 25X/6 = 1200/X + 75X/18.
Check at X = 60: 20 + 250 = 270 paise per km. The photo's tick on (c) is a per-hour figure with a wrong constant.

**Concept: direct variation and converting "per hour" to "per km".** "A varies as B²" means A = kB² for a fixed k; find k from the one data point given. To turn a cost per hour into a cost per km, divide by the distance covered in an hour, which is the speed. Watch the units: rupees to paise (× 100).

### 5 · Q5, flashing light

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

If a light flashes every 6 seconds, how many times will it flash in $\tfrac34$ of an
hour?

- **(a)** 350
- **(b)** 425
- **(c)** 451
- **(d)** 450

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) 451**

**Why.** Three quarters of an hour is 45 × 60 = 2700 seconds. A flash every 6 seconds gives 2700/6 = 450 gaps. If the light flashes at the very start, the flashes sit at 0, 6, 12, ..., 2700 seconds, which is 450 + 1 = 451 flashes. (The photo's tick, 450, counts the gaps, not the flashes.)

**Concept: fence-post counting.** Posts and gaps differ by one. A 10 m fence with posts every 1 m has 10 gaps but 11 posts. Whenever events happen "every t seconds" over a stretch that includes both ends, events = (length ÷ t) + 1. The standard key for this question uses that.

### 6 · Q14 (other set), approximate the value

*Asked by: MAQ Software (TalentDecrypt test, 2025)*

Find the value of $49 / 212 \times 199 - 206 + 195$.

- **(a)** 37
- **(b)** 35
- **(c)** 32
- **(d)** 41
- **(e)** 39

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) 35**

**Why.** Do the division and multiplication first (BODMAS): 49/212 × 199 = 49 × 199/212 ≈ 9751/212 ≈ 45.99. Then 45.99 − 206 + 195 ≈ 34.99, which rounds to 35.

**Concept: order of operations and estimating.** Division and multiplication come before subtraction and addition, worked left to right. In approximation questions, notice that 199/212 is a bit less than 1, so the product is a bit less than 49: about 46. Then 46 − 11 = 35 without any long division.

### 7 · Q13, length of a fish

*Asked by: MAQ Software (TalentDecrypt test, 2025)*

A fish's head is 10 cm long; its tail is the length of the head plus one third of the
length of the body; the body equals the head plus twice the tail. What is the fish's total
length?

- **(a)** 140 cm
- **(b)** 160 cm
- **(c)** 90 cm
- **(d)** 40 cm

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 140 cm**

**Why.** Let the body be B and the tail T. Head = 10.
1. Tail = head + B/3: T = 10 + B/3.
2. Body = head + 2 × tail: B = 10 + 2T = 10 + 20 + 2B/3 = 30 + 2B/3.
3. So B/3 = 30, B = 90, and T = 10 + 30 = 40.
4. Total = 10 + 90 + 40 = 140 cm.

**Concept: substitution in linked equations.** When one unknown is defined in terms of another, put that expression straight into the other equation. You get one equation in one unknown. Collect the unknown on one side (B − 2B/3 = B/3) and solve.

### 8 · Q2, sum and difference

*Asked by: NVIDIA (SSE NCG, Nov 2024)*

$A$ and $B$ are positive integers with $A > B$. Their sum equals five times their
difference, and their product is 4 times their quotient $A/B$. Find the sum of their
squares.

- **(a)** 13
- **(b)** 20
- **(c)** 29
- **(d)** None of the above

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 13**

**Why.**
1. Sum = 5 × difference: A + B = 5A − 5B, so 6B = 4A, i.e. A = 3B/2.
2. Product = 4 × quotient: AB = 4A/B. Divide both sides by A: B = 4/B, so B² = 4 and B = 2.
3. Then A = 3. A² + B² = 9 + 4 = 13.

**Concept: cancel a common factor instead of expanding.** AB = 4A/B has A on both sides; dividing by A (allowed because A is positive) removes it at once. Turning "sum is five times difference" into a ratio A : B = 3 : 2 is the other quick step.

### 9 · Q4, sum of cubes

*Asked by: NVIDIA (SSE NCG, Nov 2024)*

The sum of two numbers is 14 and the sum of their cubes is 1736. What is the absolute
value of their difference?

- **(a)** 6
- **(b)** 8
- **(c)** 10
- **(d)** None of the above

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) 10**

**Why.**
1. Use a³ + b³ = (a + b)³ − 3ab(a + b). With a + b = 14: 1736 = 2744 − 42ab, so ab = 24.
2. Then (a − b)² = (a + b)² − 4ab = 196 − 96 = 100, so |a − b| = 10. (The numbers are 12 and 2.)

**Concept: symmetric-sum identities.** Many questions give you a + b and some other combination. Rewrite everything in terms of the sum s = a + b and the product p = ab:
- a² + b² = s² − 2p
- a³ + b³ = s³ − 3ps
- (a − b)² = s² − 4p
Find p from the given data, then read off whatever is asked.

The distance covered by a particle in $t$ seconds consists of two parts, one of which varies

### 11 · number line

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

$-3.4$ is a number on the real number line. If we subtract 1 from it, the new number will
be

- **(a)** Farther from the origin than $-3.4$
- **(b)** Closer to the origin than $-3.4$
- **(c)** Equally far from the origin as $-3.4$
- **(d)** None of the above

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) farther from the origin than −3.4**

**Why.** −3.4 − 1 = −4.4. Distance from 0 is the absolute value: |−4.4| = 4.4 > 3.4.

**Concept: absolute value as distance.** On the number line, |x| is how far x is from 0, ignoring direction. Subtracting moves you left. For a negative number, moving left takes you further from 0; for a positive number it brings you closer (until you cross 0).

### 12 · difference of squares

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

The difference of two numbers is 8 and the difference of their squares is 160. The
numbers are

- **(a)** 18, 10
- **(b)** 8, 16
- **(c)** 6, 14
- **(d)** None of the above

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) 6 and 14**

**Why.** a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b), so 160 = 8 × (a + b) and a + b = 20. With a − b = 8: a = 14, b = 6. Check: 196 − 36 = 160.

**Concept: difference of squares.** a² − b² always factors as (a − b)(a + b). If you know the difference of squares and the plain difference, dividing gives the sum. Sum and difference together give both numbers: the bigger is (sum + difference)/2.

### 13 · Alto owners

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

In a class of 80 students, $\tfrac45$ own cars. If $\tfrac{15}{16}$ of them own an Alto,
how many own an Alto?

- **(a)** 64
- **(b)** 20
- **(c)** 60
- **(d)** Data Inconsistent

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) 60**

**Why.** Car owners = 4/5 × 80 = 64. Alto owners = 15/16 × 64 = 60.

**Concept: "a fraction of a fraction" means multiply.** "15/16 of them" refers to the car owners, not the whole class, so apply the second fraction to the result of the first. Equivalently 80 × 4/5 × 15/16 = 60.
