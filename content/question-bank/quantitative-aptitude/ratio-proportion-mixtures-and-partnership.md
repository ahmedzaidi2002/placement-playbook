### 1 · prices of apples, oranges and bananas

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

The prices of apples, oranges and bananas were in the ratio 3:5:7 in 2010 and 6:7:8 in
2022. The price of apples increased by 230% from 2010 to 2022. If the price of oranges in
2010 was ₹100, what is the price of oranges in 2022? (The small print is blurred; this is
the most legible reading.)

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: ₹231** (reconstructed from a blurred photo; one option appears to read 231)

**Why.**
1. In 2010 apples : oranges = 3 : 5 and oranges = 100, so apples = 60.
2. Apples rose 230%: 60 × 3.3 = 198 in 2022.
3. In 2022 apples : oranges = 6 : 7, so oranges = 198 × 7/6 = 231.

**Concept: "increased by 230%" means multiply by 3.3, not 2.3.** An increase of p% multiplies by (1 + p/100). Use one known price to fix each year's ratio scale.

### 2 · Q7, proportional scores

*Asked by: Grey Orange (OA, Oct 2024)*

A student took four examinations, each worth the same number of points. The marks were
in the proportion $1 : 2 : 3 : 4$. In the first three examinations together, the
student obtained 50% of the total points. On how many exams did the student score more
than 11.11%?

- **(a)** 1
- **(b)** 2
- **(c)** 3
- **(d)** 4

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) 4**

**Why.** Each exam is worth M. Marks are k, 2k, 3k, 4k. "50% of the total points in the first three exams" means 6k = 0.5 × 3M, so k = M/4. Scores: 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%, all above 11.11%. (Reading it as 50% of the student's own total is impossible: the first three hold 6/10 of that.)

**Concept: ratio with a hidden scale.** Write ratio parts as k, 2k, 3k, ...; one extra fact fixes k. When a reading leads to a contradiction, the other reading is the intended one.

### 3 · Q6, partnership

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

A, B and C start a business. A invests Rs 6500 for 6 months, B Rs 8400 for 5 months
and C Rs 10{,}000 for 3 months. A is the working member and receives 5% of the
profits for it. The profit earned is Rs 7400. Calculate B's share of the profit.

- **(a)** 2660
- **(b)** 1900
- **(c)** 2800
- **(d)** 2840

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) Rs 2660**

**Why.**
1. A first takes 5% of 7400 = 370 for working, leaving 7030.
2. Share by capital × time: A 6500 × 6 = 39,000; B 8400 × 5 = 42,000; C 10,000 × 3 = 30,000. Ratio 39 : 42 : 30 (sum 111).
3. B's share = 7030 × 42/111 = 2660.

**Concept: partnership shares.** Profit is split in proportion to capital × time (money invested for longer earns more). Any salary or commission to a working partner is taken out first, then the rest is split.

### 4 · Q24, mixing tea

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

In what ratio should a shopkeeper mix tea costing Rs 120 per kg and Rs 180 per kg so
that by selling the mixture at Rs 205 per kg he earns a profit of 25%?

- **(a)** 2:5
- **(b)** 4:11
- **(c)** 3:8
- **(d)** 4:15
- **(e)** 3:7

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) 4 : 11**

**Why.** Selling at 205 with 25% profit means cost = 205/1.25 = 164 per kg. Alligation: cheap : dear = (180 − 164) : (164 − 120) = 16 : 44 = 4 : 11.

**Concept: alligation.** To mix two prices a < b into an average m, use quantities in the ratio (b − m) : (m − a). Each ingredient's share is the distance of the **other** price from the mean. It follows from the weighted average a·x + b·y = m(x + y).

### 5 · Q7, mixing alloys

*Asked by: Qualcomm (Nov 2024 (HirePro))*

In two alloys the ratio of iron to copper is $4:3$ and $6:1$. If 14 kg of the first alloy
[cut off in the source] ... alloy?

- **(a)** 11:8
- **(b)** 8:1
- **(c)** 11:3
- **(d)** 3:11

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) 11 : 3** (the usual version of this question; the photo is cut off, and its tick on 3 : 11 is the inverted ratio)

**Why.** Usual data: 14 kg of alloy 1 (iron : copper = 4 : 3) mixed with 42 kg of alloy 2 (6 : 1).
- Alloy 1: iron 8, copper 6. Alloy 2: iron 36, copper 6.
- Total iron 44, copper 12: 44 : 12 = 11 : 3.

**Concept: mix by components.** Convert each alloy into actual weights of each metal using its ratio, add metal by metal, and form the new ratio. Do not add ratios directly.

### 6 · Problem Solving

*Asked by: Qualcomm (60-question online assessment)*

A 70-litre solution contains liquids P and Q in the ratio 3:4. The solution is divided into two parts in the ratio 2:3. To the smaller part, 4 litres of pure P is added. To the larger part, 6 litres of pure Q is added. The two parts are then mixed again. What is the final ratio of P to Q?

- **(a)** 16 : 23
- **(b)** 23 : 17
- **(c)** 18 : 23
- **(d)** 17 : 23

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) 17 : 23**

**Why.** 70 L at 3 : 4 is P 30, Q 40. Split 2 : 3 into 28 L (P 12, Q 16) and 42 L (P 18, Q 24); each part keeps the 3 : 4 ratio. Add 4 L P to the smaller: P 16, Q 16. Add 6 L Q to the larger: P 18, Q 30. Mix: P 34, Q 46 = 17 : 23.

**Concept: splitting a mixture keeps its ratio.** Any portion of a well-mixed solution has the same composition. Track each component in litres; at the end the split does not even matter (total P = 30 + 4, total Q = 40 + 6).

### 7 · C5, salaries in the ratio 4 to 5

*Asked by: Siemens Energy (MNNIT Allahabad (Mettl))*

The salaries of P and Q are in the ratio 4:5. If P gets a 25% increment, his salary
becomes \$1500. What is Q's current salary?

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: \$1500**

**Why.** After a 25% increment P earns 1500, so now P earns 1500/1.25 = 1200. Q : P = 5 : 4, so Q = 1200 × 5/4 = 1500.

**Concept: undo the percentage first.** Find the current value by dividing by the multiplier, then apply the ratio.

### 8 · a bakery's stock

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (BITS Pilani)*

Three items are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$ in 2020. Removing $X$ of each gives $3 : 5 : 7$ in
2021; removing 10 more of each gives $1 : 2 : 3$ in 2022. Total removed in 2021?

- **(a)** 35
- **(b)** 30
- **(c)** 40
- **(d)** 45

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) 30**

**Why.** Let the 2020 stock be 2k, 3k, 4k.
1. After removing X of each: (2k − X) : (3k − X) = 3 : 5 → 10k − 5X = 9k − 3X → k = 2X.
2. After removing 10 more: (2k − X − 10) : (3k − X − 10) = 1 : 2 → 4k − 2X − 20 = 3k − X − 10 → k = X + 10.
3. So 2X = X + 10 → X = 10. Total removed in 2021 = 3X = 30.

**Concept: ratio equations by cross-multiplying.** a : b = c : d means ad = bc. Each new ratio gives one equation; two equations fix both unknowns.

### 9 · a mocktail

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IISc, second set)*

15 litres hold water and mocktail mix in the ratio $10 : 5$. What fraction of the mixture
must be drawn out and replaced by mix to make it half and half?

- **(a)** 1/4
- **(b)** 1/8
- **(c)** 1/2
- **(d)** 1/3

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 1/4**

**Why.** 15 L with water 10, mix 5. Draw x litres: water left = 10 − (2/3)x (water is 2/3 of anything drawn). Refill with mix keeps 15 L. Half and half needs water = 7.5: 10 − 2x/3 = 7.5 → x = 3.75 = 1/4 of 15. (The NIT candidate's 1/3 is wrong.)

**Concept: replacement problems.** Drawing out a portion removes each component in proportion to its share. Adding back one component only changes that one. Track the component whose amount you can follow most easily.

### 10 · sharing plants

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IISc 2023)*

John : Ben $= 8 : 15$. After Ben gives some plants to John it is $6 : 11$. What fraction of his
plants did Ben give?

- **(a)** 2/75
- **(b)** 1/50
- **(c)** 3/15
- **(d)** 2/5

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: no option matches; by the stated numbers Ben gave 2/255 of his plants.**

**Why.** John 8k, Ben 15k. Ben gives x: (8k + x)/(15k − x) = 6/11 → 88k + 11x = 90k − 6x → x = 2k/17. As a fraction of Ben's 15k: 2/255. None of the options equals this, so the question was probably mis-transcribed.

**Concept: transfers keep the total fixed.** Total 23k stays the same; the new ratio 6 : 11 has 17 parts, which does not divide 23 evenly, a sign that the printed numbers are off. The method (set up the new ratio and cross-multiply) is what to learn.

### 11 · sharing grazing rent

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IISc 2023)*

Jerry: 30 buffaloes for 3 months and 15 cows for 2; Gonzales: 20 cows for 3 months and 45 goats
for $4\tfrac25$; 10 buffaloes eat as 5 cows and 6 cows as 10 goats. Gonzales's share?

- **(a)** 3/5
- **(b)** 5/12
- **(c)** 1/5
- **(d)** 4/5

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: no option matches; with the stated data Gonzales pays about 70%.**

**Why.** Convert everything to cow-months. 10 buffaloes eat as 5 cows (1 buffalo = 0.5 cow); 6 cows eat as 10 goats (1 goat = 0.6 cow).
- Jerry: 30 buffaloes × 3 = 90 buffalo-months = 45 cow-months; 15 cows × 2 = 30. Total 75.
- Gonzales: 20 cows × 3 = 60; 45 goats × 4.4 = 198 goat-months = 118.8 cow-months. Total 178.8.
- Share = 178.8/253.8 ≈ 0.70.

**Concept: rent in proportion to consumption × time.** Shared-pasture questions turn every animal into one common unit (here cow-months), then split the rent in that ratio. The options suggest a transcription error in the source.

### 12 · water in milk

*Asked by: Texas Instruments (IIT ISM 2024)*

In what ratio must water be mixed with milk to gain $14\tfrac23$% selling at cost price?

- **(a)** 7:25
- **(b)** 3:25
- **(c)** 6:25
- **(d)** 11:75

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) 11 : 75**

**Why.** Selling at cost price, the gain comes only from the free water. A gain of 14 2/3% = 44/3 % means for every 100 units of milk paid for, 44/3 units of water are sold as milk. Water : milk = 44/3 : 100 = 44 : 300 = 11 : 75.

**Concept: gain from adding a free ingredient.** If you sell at the cost price of milk, profit % = water/milk × 100. So water : milk = gain% : 100.

### 13 · proportional rectangle

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

The length and breadth of a rectangle are directly proportional. If the length increases
from 6 cm to 21 cm and the breadth is now 14 cm, what was the breadth before the
change?

- **(a)** 4 cm
- **(b)** 1.5 cm
- **(c)** 2 cm
- **(d)** 3 cm

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 4 cm**

**Why.** Directly proportional means breadth/length is constant. Now 14/21 = 2/3, so before: 6 × 2/3 = 4 cm.

**Concept: direct proportion.** y ∝ x means y = kx, so y/x is the same for every pair. Find k from the known pair and apply it to the other.
