### 1 · sentence correction, calculators

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Improve the underlined part, if needed: "We did not stop children from doing arithmetic by
hand even though electronic calculators <u>was available in the past as well</u>."

- **(a)** was wasted in the history too
- **(b)** were available decades ago
- **(c)** were there in the earlier years of time
- **(d)** was available in the past as well

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) were available decades ago**

**Why.** The subject "calculators" is plural, so the verb must be "were" (rules out (a) and (d)). (c) is wordy ("earlier years of time"). (b) is grammatical and keeps the meaning.

**Concept: subject–verb agreement.** A plural subject takes a plural verb (they were, calculators were); a singular subject takes a singular verb. Find the real subject first, ignoring phrases in between.

### 2 · error detection, the economists

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Choose the wrong phrase: "The economics of the third world countries (A) has always
baffled (B) most economists who were, (C) in fact, worried about the future (D)."

- **(a)** B
- **(b)** D
- **(c)** C
- **(d)** A

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) C**

**Why.** "Has always baffled" (present perfect) describes something still true now, so the economists "are" worried, not "were". The tense in part C clashes with the rest.

**Concept: tense consistency.** The present perfect ("has baffled") links the past to now. A clause describing the same ongoing situation should stay in the present.

### 3 · error detection, the forest

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Choose the wrong phrase: "Vast expenses of (A) the forest have succumbed from (B) forest
fires in the (C) past five years (D)."

- **(a)** B
- **(b)** D
- **(c)** A
- **(d)** C

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) B**

**Why.** The correct idiom is "succumb **to**" (fires), not "succumb from". (Part A also has a spelling slip, "expenses" for "expanses", but the intended error is the preposition.)

**Concept: fixed prepositions.** Many verbs take one particular preposition: succumb to, comply with, depend on, consist of. These have to be learned as pairs.

### 4 · sentence correction, the tour itinerary

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Improve the underlined part, if needed: "<u>Given that the tour itinerary
involved</u> a full week, our travel party included two couples along with two toddlers."

- **(a)** The tour itinerary is involving
- **(b)** As the tour itinerary will involve
- **(c)** Touring itinerary involved
- **(d)** Given that the tour itinerary involved

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) no change**

**Why.** "Given that the tour itinerary involved a full week" is a correct past-tense reason clause matching "our travel party included". (a) and (b) use the wrong tense; (c) drops "the" and the linking word.

**Concept: "no correction" is a real option.** If the original is grammatical and clear, do not change it. Check tense agreement between the clauses first.

### 5 · sentence correction, a regulatory framework

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Improve the underlined part: "A regulatory framework that <u>balance the free
speech and accountability are</u> one of the hallmarks of a mature democracy."

- **(a)** will balance accountability and free speech as
- **(b)** balance the free speech and accountability are
- **(c)** balances free speech and accountability is
- **(d)** can balance free speech and accountability to be

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) balances free speech and accountability is**

**Why.** The subject is "a regulatory framework" (singular), so both verbs are singular: "balances" and "is". "Free speech" is an uncountable general idea and takes no article.

**Concept: agreement inside relative clauses.** In "a framework that balances", the verb after "that" agrees with "framework". A long subject ("A framework that ... accountability") still needs a singular main verb.

### 6 · error detection, the thieves

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Choose the wrong phrase: "By the (A) time they have got (B) back home (C) the thieves had
escaped (D)."

- **(a)** B
- **(b)** D
- **(c)** C
- **(d)** A

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) B**

**Why.** "By the time they got back home, the thieves had escaped." The sentence is set in the past (had escaped), so part B must be past ("got"), not present perfect ("have got").

**Concept: past perfect sequencing.** Use the past perfect (had escaped) for the earlier of two past events, and the simple past (got back) for the later one.

### 7 · Q4

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

Choose the option that conforms most closely to Standard English usage.

- **(a)** We are forced to fall back on fatalism as an explanation of irrational events.
- **(b)** We are forced to falling back on the fatalism as an explanation of irrational events.
- **(c)** We are forced to fall back to fatalism as an explanation of irrational events.
- **(d)** None of the above.
- **(e)** We are forced to fall back on fatalism as explanations of irrational events.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a)**

**Why.** The idiom is "fall back **on**", "forced to" takes the base verb ("fall", not "falling"), fatalism is "an explanation" (singular), and "the fatalism" adds an unneeded article.

**Concept: infinitives and idioms.** After "to" (as in "forced to") use the base form of the verb. Abstract nouns like fatalism normally take no article.

### 8 · Q9

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

Choose the option that conforms most closely to Standard English usage.

- **(a)** The running of a large business consists of getting somebody to sell something that somebody else made for more than it cost.
- **(b)** The running of large businesses consist of getting somebody to make something that somebody else sold to somebody else for more than its cost.
- **(c)** The running of large businesses consist of getting somebody to make something else that somebody else will sell to somebody else for more than it costs.
- **(d)** None of the above.
- **(e)** The running of a large business consists of getting somebody to make something that somebody else will sell to somebody else for more than it costs.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (e)**

**Why.** The subject "the running" is singular, so the verb is "consists" (rules out (b), (c)). (a) changes the meaning and mixes tenses. (e) keeps agreement and consistent tenses ("will sell ... costs"). The photo's (c) has the agreement error.

**Concept: gerund subjects are singular.** "The running of large businesses" is one activity, even though "businesses" is plural. The verb agrees with "running".

### 9 · Q10, sentence correction

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

NASA reports that ancient observers took note of solar eclipses at least as far back
as 2500 BC, *as evidenced by surviving records from* ancient Babylon and China.

- **(a)** as evidenced by records that have survived in
- **(b)** No correction required
- **(c)** as the evidence that has survived from the records of
- **(d)** as shows the evidence of records surviving in
- **(e)** as the surviving records show the evidence of

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) no correction required**

**Why.** "As evidenced by surviving records from ancient Babylon and China" correctly introduces evidence for the claim. (a) says the records survived **in** Babylon (they come **from** it), changing the meaning; (c)–(e) are wordy or ungrammatical.

**Concept: "as evidenced by".** This fixed phrase means "as shown by". Keep the original when it is concise and accurate.

### 10 · Q11, sentence correction

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

*It was until many years* later that Gandhi became a rebel against authority.

- **(a)** It was not until many years
- **(b)** It was not many years
- **(c)** No correction required
- **(d)** Until it was many years
- **(e)** It was till many years

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) It was not until many years**

**Why.** The idiom is "It was **not until** X that Y happened", meaning Y happened only after X.

**Concept: "not until" construction.** "It was not until 1990 that ..." emphasises lateness. Without "not" the sentence says the opposite of what is meant.

### 11 · Q14, spot the error

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

Choose the option which contains a mistake. (The parts form the sentence
"This has slowed the progress / of reforms in many countries / because the choice of
either of the extreme / positions inevitably invite criticism.")

- **(a)** because the choice of either of the extreme
- **(b)** None of the above
- **(c)** positions inevitably invite criticism.
- **(d)** of reforms in many countries
- **(e)** This has slowed the progress

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c)**

**Why.** The subject of "invite" is "the choice" (singular), so it must be "invites criticism". "Positions" is just next to the verb, not its subject.

**Concept: the proximity trap.** A plural noun right before the verb (positions) can mislead you. Trace back to the head noun of the subject (the choice ... of ... positions).

### 12 · Q15, sentence correction

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

The farmer *was fool apparently* to believe him.

- **(a)** was a fool apparently
- **(b)** was apparently a fool
- **(c)** was apparently fool
- **(d)** apparently was a fool
- **(e)** No correction required

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) was apparently a fool**

**Why.** "Fool" is a countable noun and needs the article "a". The adverb "apparently" fits naturally between "was" and its complement.

**Concept: countable nouns need an article in the singular.** "He is a doctor", "She was a fool". Adverbs usually go after the verb "to be".

### 13 · Q22, sentence correction

*Asked by: KLA (IIT Delhi 2023)*

There is no doubt *whether he will be* punished for his wrong doings.

- **(a)** that he will be
- **(b)** whether he would be
- **(c)** that he has
- **(d)** No correction required
- **(e)** whether he is

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) that he will be**

**Why.** "There is no doubt" is followed by "that". "Doubt whether" is used only when there is real doubt.

**Concept: "no doubt that" vs "doubt whether".** Certainty → "that"; uncertainty → "whether/if". "I doubt whether he will come" but "There is no doubt that he will come".

### 17 · sentence improvement

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Improve the part in italics: A writer *always is having* his own vision of life.

- **(a)** always has
- **(b)** always had
- **(c)** always have
- **(d)** have always
- **(e)** no improvement needed

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) always has**

**Why.** "Have" meaning "possess" is a state, not an action, so it does not take the -ing form. The singular subject "a writer" takes "has".

**Concept: stative verbs.** Verbs of state (have = own, know, believe, like) are not used in continuous tenses: "I know", not "I am knowing".
