### 1 · fill in, the committee's decision

*Asked by: EXL (HackerEarth test, 2025)*

The committee's decision was met with considerable _____; some members argued that it
was _____ and lacked thorough analysis.

- **(a)** acclaim, comprehensive
- **(b)** opposition, hasty
- **(c)** indifference, deliberate
- **(d)** approval, prudent

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) opposition, hasty**

**Why.** "Lacked thorough analysis" is a criticism, so the decision was "hasty", and a criticised decision meets "opposition".

**Concept: use clue words for tone.** Find the part of the sentence that fixes the meaning ("lacked thorough analysis") and pick words with the same positive or negative tone.

### 2 · curious and judging

*Asked by: EXL (HackerEarth test, 2025)*

It is not possible to _____ and stay curious simultaneously.

- **(a)** Judge
- **(b)** Disagree
- **(c)** Understand
- **(d)** Argue

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) judge**

**Why.** Curiosity means keeping an open mind; judging means deciding. You cannot do both about the same thing at once.

**Concept: logical opposites in fill-ins.** "Not possible to X and Y simultaneously" asks for an X that contradicts Y.

### 3 · fill in, groundbreaking research

*Asked by: EXL (HackerEarth test, 2025)*

The scientist's groundbreaking research was _____ by many in the academic community,
although a few critics _____ its methodology.

- **(a)** lauded, questioned
- **(b)** ignored, praised
- **(c)** condemned, admired
- **(d)** dismissed, embraced

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) lauded, questioned**

**Why.** "Although" sets up a contrast: many praised it (lauded), but a few critics questioned its method.

**Concept: contrast signals.** Although, but, yet, however require the two blanks to point in opposite directions.

### 4 · fill in, ... she studied well

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Fill in one word: _____ she studied well, she failed the test.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: Although** (or "Though")

**Why.** Studying well and failing are opposites, so the clause needs a concession word.

**Concept: concession clauses.** "Although X, Y" means Y happened despite X.

### 5 · fill in, even ... it didn't cost a lot

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Fill in one word: Even _____ it didn't cost a lot, buying the gadget would be wasteful.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: though**

**Why.** "Even though" is a fixed phrase meaning "despite the fact that".

**Concept: "even though" vs "even if".** "Even though" is for a fact (it really didn't cost much); "even if" is for a possibility.

### 6 · fill in, never ... danced

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Fill in the blank (one word): Never _____ danced before, she was very nervous at the
audition.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: having**

**Why.** "Never having danced before, she was nervous" is a participle clause giving the reason, with "having + past participle" for an earlier time.

**Concept: perfect participle.** "Having done X" shows X happened (or did not happen) before the main action and often explains it.

### 7 · fill in, left with no ...

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Fill in one word: He was left with no _____ but to accept the terms and conditions.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: choice** (or "option", "alternative")

**Why.** "Left with no choice but to ..." is the fixed expression.

**Concept: collocations.** Some words go together by habit: "no choice but to", "make a decision", "take a risk".

### 8 · fill in, six years ...

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Fill in the blank (one word): She could not remember details from the incident that had
taken place six years _____.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: before** (or "earlier")

**Why.** With the past perfect ("had taken place"), the time is counted back from a past moment, so "before". "Ago" counts back from now and goes with the simple past.

**Concept: "ago" vs "before".** "Six years ago" = six years before now. "Six years before" = six years before a past reference point.

### 9 · fill the gap (8 items)

*Asked by: Schindler (Aon English test)*

Choose the option that fills the gap.

1. There are times when you have to force yourself to improve your performance, and
    that's simply called self-___. - mastery, manifestation, movement, motivation
2. I am writing ___ you of my change of address. - for to inform, to informing,
    to inform, for informing
3. There was no fixed agenda for that particular day as it was to be regarded simply
    as a ___ meeting. - unplanned, casual, possible, probable
4. Thanks for your help. `___' - You're welcome., Never mind., It doesn't
    matter., Oh well.
5. If you want to try and influence that political party, it's best to become a member
    yourself and then you can argue from the ___. - beginning, side, inside, entrance
6. Feel free to visit anything of interest to you in the exhibition, and also, you can
    ___ of any of the refreshments available. - undertake, partake, retake, intake
7. What ___ of time would you stay with us if we offered you the job? - duration,
    long, length, span
8. She is very happy because she starts her new ___ today. - labour, worked,
    postponed, job

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answers: (1) motivation (2) to inform (3) casual (4) You're welcome (5) inside (6) partake (7) length (8) job**

**Why.**
1. Self-motivation means pushing yourself.
2. "I am writing to inform you": a purpose infinitive.
3. No fixed agenda → a casual meeting.
4. The standard reply to "thanks" is "You're welcome".
5. "Argue from the inside" = as a member.
6. "Partake of" refreshments = eat or drink some.
7. "What length of time"; "duration" does not take "of time".
8. "Starts her new job".

**Concept: collocations and fixed phrases.** Many of these test which word naturally goes with another (partake of, length of time). Read the whole sentence and choose the word that sounds like a set phrase.

### 10 · D1, fill in, much ... with the change

*Asked by: Siemens Energy (MNNIT Allahabad (Mettl))*

Fill in the blank: She was much _____ with the change in his behaviour. (The options
are off screen.)

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: pleased** (or "impressed", "annoyed")

**Why.** The gap needs an adjective that takes "with" (pleased with, impressed with). "Much" can go before a past participle used as an adjective ("much pleased").

**Concept: adjective + preposition pairs.** pleased with, angry with, interested in, afraid of.

### 11 · fill in the blank

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Fill in the blank: There are many textile mills in the market that compete with each
______ to gain the largest share of the market.

- **(a)** person
- **(b)** other
- **(c)** contestants
- **(d)** individual

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) other**

**Why.** "Compete with each other" uses the reciprocal pronoun "each other": the mills compete with one another.

**Concept: reciprocal pronouns.** "Each other" / "one another" show a two-way action between members of a group.

### 12 · fill in the blank

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Fill in the blank: All members will have a fixed five-year ______.

- **(a)** tenet
- **(b)** tenor
- **(c)** tenure
- **(d)** tenement

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) tenure**

**Why.** Tenure is a period of holding an office. A tenet is a principle; tenor is the general meaning (or a singing voice); a tenement is a building of flats.

**Concept: confusable words.** Words that look alike often have unrelated meanings. Learn them in groups.

### 13 · fill in the blank

*Asked by: Wells Fargo (SHL test, Oct 2022)*

Fill in the blank: Movies are becoming a medium of cultural contacts, good relations and
______ among different countries.

- **(a)** wars
- **(b)** love
- **(c)** harmony
- **(d)** conformity

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) harmony**

**Why.** The list is positive (cultural contacts, good relations), and "harmony among countries" is the natural phrase. "Wars" contradicts the tone; "conformity" means sameness, not friendship.

**Concept: parallel lists keep the same tone.** All items in a list joined by "and" should belong together.
