### 1 · Q24, one word for the study of populations

*Asked by: EPAM (EPAM freshers test)*

Choose the single word for "the scientific study of human populations, with statistics
of births, deaths, etc."

- **(a)** Demographics
- **(b)** Ethnology
- **(c)** Demography
- **(d)** Topology

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) Demography**

**Why.** Demography is the study itself. Demographics are the statistical data; ethnology studies cultures; topology is a branch of maths.

**Concept: -graphy vs -graphics.** "-graphy" names a field of study or description (geography, demography); the plural "-ics" form often names the data or features.

### 2 · Q25, eat humble pie

*Asked by: EPAM (EPAM freshers test)*

"The PR consultant advised the politician to *eat humble pie* and apologise before the
story went viral." The idiom means:

- **(a)** Deny all allegations
- **(b)** Offer monetary compensation
- **(c)** Issue a formal apology
- **(d)** Divert media attention

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) issue a formal apology**

**Why.** To "eat humble pie" is to admit a mistake and apologise humbly.

**Concept: idioms.** Idioms mean something different from their words. Learn them with an example sentence.

### 3 · one-word substitution, different languages

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

One word for: one who enables people speaking different languages to understand each other.
(Visible options include Transcriber and Transmitter.)

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: Interpreter**

**Why.** An interpreter helps people speaking different languages understand each other in conversation. A translator works on written text; a transcriber writes down speech; a transmitter sends signals.

**Concept: one-word substitution.** Pick the word whose exact definition matches every part of the description (spoken, between languages).

### 4 · one-word substitution

*Asked by: Oracle (HackerRank MCQ test)*

Choose the word that can be substituted for: "A self-governing country or region".

- **(a)** Autonomy
- **(b)** Autocracy
- **(c)** Anarchy
- **(d)** Ethnology

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) Autonomy**

**Why.** Autonomy is self-government. Autocracy is rule by one person; anarchy is the absence of government; ethnology is the study of peoples.

**Concept: Greek roots.** auto- = self, -nomy = law/rule (autonomy = self-rule); -cracy = power (autocracy = power of one); an- = without, -archy = rule (anarchy = no rule).

### 5 · idiom (2)

*Asked by: Oracle (HackerRank MCQ test)*

I've been studying for this exam for months, so I'm hoping to ______.

- **(a)** Hit the ground running
- **(b)** Have a field day
- **(c)** Be a dark horse
- **(d)** Have a lot on my plate

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) have a field day** is the closest fit; (a) "hit the ground running" is also often keyed.

**Why.** "Have a field day" means to enjoy great success; after months of study you hope to do very well. "Hit the ground running" means start something new at full speed. "A dark horse" is an unexpected winner (contradicts months of preparation); "a lot on my plate" means being busy.

**Concept: choose the idiom whose meaning fits the context.** Check each idiom's real meaning against the situation; when none is perfect, pick the closest.

### 6 · idiom (1)

*Asked by: Oracle (HackerRank MCQ test)*

I don't think we should hire him. He seems like ______.

- **(a)** A fish out of water
- **(b)** The black sheep of the family
- **(c)** A wolf in sheep's clothing
- **(d)** The apple of my eye

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) a wolf in sheep's clothing**

**Why.** Someone who seems harmless but is dangerous is a good reason not to hire him. "A fish out of water" means uncomfortable, a weaker reason.

**Concept: idioms of appearance vs reality.** "Wolf in sheep's clothing" = hidden danger; "black sheep" = the disgraced member of a group; "apple of my eye" = a favourite.
