### 1 · Q26, order the sentences on focus

*Asked by: EPAM (EPAM freshers test)*

Order the sentences into a coherent paragraph. (1) The ability to maintain an unwavering
focus on one's goals is often regarded as a cornerstone of achieving success. (2) The path
to success is usually paved with consistent effort and determination, requiring individuals
to stay committed over time. (3) The presence of distractions, however, can significantly
hinder progress, making it harder to stay on course. (4) A critical aspect of ensuring
success lies in actively identifying and eliminating distractions that serve no
constructive purpose.

- **(a)** 1-2-3-4
- **(b)** 3-4-1-2
- **(c)** 2-1-3-4
- **(d)** 1-3-4-2

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (a) 1-2-3-4**

**Why.** 1 states the main idea (focus leads to success), 2 expands it (consistent effort), 3 introduces the obstacle with "however" (distractions), and 4 gives the remedy (remove distractions).

**Concept: paragraph flow.** A paragraph usually goes: topic sentence → development → contrast/problem → solution or conclusion. Words like "however" must follow the idea they contrast with.

### 2 · paragraph order, great apes

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Order the sentences: A. They have expectations about how they should be treated by others
and will publicly protest when treated unfairly. B. Research has shown that great apes
possess certain expectations about social regularities and both perceive and act according
to social rules within their group. C. Protesting behaviours were generally seen among
those whose expectations about a social situation were violated. D. Bonobos are our closest
living relatives, along with chimpanzees, yet are little understood.

- **(a)** BCDA
- **(b)** DBAC
- **(c)** DACB
- **(d)** BDAC

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) DBAC**

**Why.** D introduces bonobos; B gives the research finding about great apes; A ("They have expectations...") develops it; C explains when the protests appear.

**Concept: pronoun links.** A sentence starting with "They" or "This" cannot open a paragraph; it must follow the sentence that names what "they" are.

### 3 · paragraph order, the kites

*Asked by: Flipkart (Neev 2025)*

Order the sentences: A. I was one among the army of 10-12 lads carrying bamboo sticks as
weapons. B. We were oblivious of the surroundings when I suddenly bumped into my brother.
C. He was probably on his way back home from the bazaar. D. One pleasant evening, I set
about running after colourful kites. E. He caught me by the wrist and asked tersely, `Are
you out of your mind?' F. Our mission was to collect as many fallen kites as we could.

- **(a)** ABCDEF
- **(b)** DAFBCE
- **(c)** DFBCEA
- **(d)** FBADEC

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) DAFBCE**

**Why.** D sets the scene (one evening, kites); A introduces the group; F gives its mission; B is the sudden encounter with the brother; C explains why the brother was there; E is his reaction.

**Concept: narrative order.** Stories move from setting → characters → action → event → reaction. Pronouns ("He" in C and E) need the person named first (B).

### 4 · D3, rearrange, world hunger

*Asked by: Siemens Energy (MNNIT Allahabad (Mettl))*

Rearrange (1 and 6 fixed): 1. I wish we could - (A) so that no one (B) to end world
hunger (C) have enough money (D) in the entire world - 6. has to be hungry ever again.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: CBDA**

**Why.** "I wish we could have enough money (C) to end world hunger (B) in the entire world (D) so that no one (A) has to be hungry ever again."

**Concept: grammar links between fragments.** "Could" needs a base verb (have); "so that no one" must sit right before "has to be".

### 5 · D5, rearrange, the ocean

*Asked by: Siemens Energy (MNNIT Allahabad (Mettl))*

Rearrange (1 and 6 fixed): 1. As the - A. exuded a raw, untamed beauty that left
onlookers B. sprays of salty mist into the air, the coastal landscape C. waves crashed
against the rugged cliffs, sending D. in awe of the immense power and majesty of - 6. the
ocean.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: CBAD**

**Why.** "As the waves crashed against the rugged cliffs, sending (C) sprays of salty mist into the air, the coastal landscape (B) exuded a raw, untamed beauty that left onlookers (A) in awe of the immense power and majesty of (D) the ocean."

**Concept: follow the sentence's grammar.** "As the" needs a subject (waves); "sending" needs an object (sprays); "the landscape" needs a verb (exuded); "left onlookers" continues "in awe of".
