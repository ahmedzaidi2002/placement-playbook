### 1 · ending a phone call politely

*Asked by: EXL (HackerEarth test, 2025)*

You have worked for two hours. A friend calls to ask whether you have heard Linkin Park's
new song. You do not want to talk, and you do not want to be harsh. Which sentence ends the
conversation appropriately?

- **(a)** Yes, it is good. I will go to bed anyways as I am tired.
- **(b)** Yes, it is good. Anyways, I am tired. I was working for sometime and will sleep now.
- **(c)** Yes, it is good but I am going to sleep now. I worked for long.
- **(d)** Yes, it is good. Anyway, I will go to bed now as I have been working for long and I am tired.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d)**

**Why.** It answers the question, gives a reason, ends politely, and is grammatical ("anyway", "have been working"). (a)–(c) are abrupt or have errors ("anyways", "for sometime").

**Concept: tone and correctness.** Polite closures acknowledge the other person, give a brief reason, and close. "Anyway" (not "anyways") is standard written English.

### 2 · Q12, outmoded

*Asked by: ION Group (SDE OA, Jul 2025)*

Choose the best definition of the italicised word from context: "The designer window
treatments in her house, installed 17 years ago, were *outmoded*."

- **(a)** unnecessary
- **(b)** pointless
- **(c)** out-of-date
- **(d)** worthless

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (c) out-of-date**

**Why.** "Installed 17 years ago" tells you the treatments are old-fashioned.

**Concept: context clues.** Look for nearby facts (ages, contrasts, examples) that hint at a word's meaning.

### 3 · Q13, obdurately

*Asked by: ION Group (SDE OA, Jul 2025)*

Choose the best definition from context: "Although Zachary is much too inexperienced for
the managerial position, he is a wilful young man and *obdurately* refuses to
withdraw his application."

- **(a)** foolishly
- **(b)** reluctantly
- **(c)** constantly
- **(d)** stubbornly

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (d) stubbornly**

**Why.** "Wilful" and "refuses to withdraw" point to an unyielding refusal.

**Concept: context clues of the same tone.** Words linked by "and" in a description usually share a meaning (wilful ... obdurately).

### 4 · Q14, atrophy

*Asked by: ION Group (SDE OA, Jul 2025)*

"In prolonged space flight, besides the obvious hazards of meteors, rocky debris and
radiation, astronauts will have to deal with muscle <u>atrophy</u> brought on by
weightlessness; therefore, when they return to Earth, they face a protracted period of
weight training to rebuild their strength." The word *atrophy* most nearly means:

- **(a)** pain
- **(b)** wasting
- **(c)** weakening
- **(d)** cramping

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answer: (b) wasting**

**Why.** Atrophy is the wasting away of tissue. Astronauts need a long time to "rebuild" strength, showing muscle was lost, not just weakened.

**Concept: precise vs loose synonyms.** Pick the option closest to the exact meaning; "weakening" is a result, "wasting" is the process.

### 5 · word for a definition (19 items)

*Asked by: Schindler (Aon English test)*

Choose the word that matches the definition.

1. unconquerable; unbeatable; invulnerable; indisputable - fortuitous, precarious,
    awry, impregnable
2. stretched tight - indomitable, robust, taut, proportionate
3. forward movement; progress; promotion - general, advancement, sign, meter
4. garland of flowers, leaves etc. hung between two points - glut, divisibility,
    festoon, demagogue
5. meticulously; exactly; scrupulously; diligently; thoroughly; in detail -
    minutely, approximately, obviously, especially
6. to publish; to distribute; to circulate; to come forth; to emit; to result from
    - issue, entrust, honor, prove
7. the act or state of lying concealed to surprise or attack the enemy - legging,
    deference, ambush, foreman
8. to implore - induct, underrate, disburden, beseech
9. unchangeable - invariable, mercenary, slothful, pestilential
10. doubtful; dubious - civic, suspect, duplicate, coordinate
11. against; in contrast to - curtly, contra, either, nevertheless
12. safety; protection; defense - security, career, import, advice
13. signed note acknowledging a debt - Inc., G.D.P., I.O.U., PTO
14. pertaining to spring - cadaverous, venerable, indefatigable, vernal
15. conclusion reached by reasoning from data or premises; speculation - candor,
    aristocracy, inference, raconteur
16. to create; to produce; to bring into being - acquire, generate, browse,
    dislodge
17. the practical unit of electric-current strength - epithet, rationalism,
    ampere, prepossession
18. absolute - subservient, similar, sheer, homogeneous
19. to annoy; to irritate - discourse, impugn, forbear, nettle

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answers: (1) impregnable (2) taut (3) advancement (4) festoon (5) minutely (6) issue (7) ambush (8) beseech (9) invariable (10) suspect (11) contra (12) security (13) I.O.U. (14) vernal (15) inference (16) generate (17) ampere (18) sheer (19) nettle**

**Why.** Each word matches its definition: impregnable = cannot be conquered; taut = stretched tight; festoon = hanging garland; minutely = in fine detail; beseech = implore; vernal = of spring; sheer = absolute ("sheer nonsense"); nettle = irritate. (One photo shows "approximately" chosen for item 5, the opposite of "minutely".)

**Concept: learn word families.** Roots help: in- (not) + variable = invariable (unchangeable); "vernal" from Latin *ver*, spring. In negative-marking tests, skip a word you don't know.

### 6 · correct spelling (6 items)

*Asked by: Schindler (Aon English test)*

Choose the right spelling: (1) merchandise / merchendise; (2) accidentally /
accidentelly; (3) indispensable / indispensible; (4) quarrel / quarrell; (5) acquisition /
aquisition; (6) indefatigable / indefatiguable.

{{< details title="Answer and explanation" closed="true" >}}

**Answers: merchandise, accidentally, indispensable, quarrel, acquisition, indefatigable**

**Why.** These are the standard spellings: "-ally" for adverbs from "-al" adjectives (accidental → accidentally), "-able" in indispensable, "acq-" in acquisition.

**Concept: common spelling patterns.** Adjectives ending in -al add -ly (accidentally, basically). "ac-q" appears in acquire/acquisition. Many "-able/-ible" words must be memorised.
